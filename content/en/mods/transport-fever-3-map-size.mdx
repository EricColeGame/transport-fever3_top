## Map Scaling Evolution: Transport Fever 2 vs. Transport Fever 3

In *Transport Fever 2*, players had access to standard map sizes ranging from Small (1:1 aspect ratio at roughly 4 km × 4 km) up to Very Large (16 km × 16 km, or up to 1:5 aspect ratios like 7 km × 36 km). PC players could also activate "experimental map sizes" via the configuration file, unlocking Megalomaniac dimensions reaching 1:1 scales of 24 km × 24 km. While these colossal maps offered unprecedented route depth, they pushed the previous engine to its technical limits, frequently resulting in late-game simulation stutter.

Evaluating the foundational **transport fever 3 map size** architecture reveals a major paradigm shift. Rather than relying on simple procedural height grids, Urban Games has rebuilt the terrain engine using node-based environmental technology. This allows the sim to render vast territories with varied regional micro-climates on a single landmass without taxing system resources in the same brute-force manner.

The table below outlines how map dimensions and aspect ratios have evolved across both titles:

| Generation / Dimension | Aspect Ratio Options | Estimated Dimensions (km) | Area (km²) | Performance Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TF2 Small** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 | 4 × 4 / 3 × 6 | ~16 | Minimal (Great for testing) |
| **TF2 Medium** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 | 6 × 6 / 4 × 8 | ~36 | Low (Entry-level rigs) |
| **TF2 Large** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 | 8 × 8 / 6 × 12 | ~64 | Moderate (Balanced play) |
| **TF2 Very Large** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:4, 1:5 | 16 × 16 / 7 × 36 | ~256 | High (Requires solid CPU) |
| **TF2 Megalomaniac (Exp.)** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:4, 1:5 | 24 × 24 / 11 × 54 | ~576 | Extreme (Heavy late-game lag) |
| **TF3 Standard Medium** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 | 8 × 8 / 6 × 12 | ~64 | Well Optimized (New baseline) |
| **TF3 Vast / Continental** | 1:1, 1:2, 1:4 | 16 × 16 / 10 × 40 | ~256–400 | Moderate to High |
| **TF3 Custom / Modded** | Dynamic Node-Bound | Up to 32 × 32 (Projected) | Up to 1,024 | Hardware Dependent |

By transitioning to node-driven layouts, players gain access to elongated corridors that provide real express-train travel times. Instead of bullet trains hitting maximum speed for only ten seconds before braking into the next terminal, higher-tier maps allow high-speed consists to run at full throttle for realistic stretches.

## Optimizing Network Layouts Across Large Dimensions

On massive maps, traditional point-to-point connections become wildly inefficient. As cities expand and resource demands scale into thousands of units per month, you must adopt tiered transit hierarchies. 

```
[Local Feeder Network] ---> [Regional Cargo Hub] ---> [Express Trunk Line] ---> [Destination City]
```

Without a structured distribution network, trains end up running half-empty across vast distances, burning operating costs with minimal returns. The table below details how to structure transit modes across different distances on expansive maps:

| Distance Range (In-Game) | Optimal Transport Mode | Recommended Rolling Stock | Infrastructure Priority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0 – 3 km (Intra-City)** | Trams, City Buses, Light Rail | High-capacity electric streetcars | Dedicated transit lanes, ring roads |
| **3 – 8 km (Suburban)** | Diesel Multiple Units (DMU) / Commuter | Rapid-acceleration trainsets | Grade-separated crossings, passing loops |
| **8 – 20 km (Intercity)** | Electric Loco-Hauled Passenger / Rail | High-speed electric locomotives | Double-tracking, block signal optimization |
| **20 – 40+ km (Cross-Map)** | High-Speed Express (Bullet Rail) | Aerodynamic trainsets (200–300+ km/h) | Dedicated bypass lines, flyover junctions |
| **Vast Water / Island Gaps** | Cargo Freighters & Regional Air | Container ships & passenger aircraft | Deep-water ports, twin-runway international airports |

Implementing hub-and-spoke configurations prevents rail congestion at central terminals. Cargo should be brought from outlying raw material nodes to consolidated distribution yards on the city perimeter before transferring to local distribution trucks.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Transport Fever 3 Map Size

### What is the maximum playable transport fever 3 map size supported by default?
Urban Games provides standard sizes scaling up to Very Large out of the box, with dimensions roughly equivalent to 16 km × 16 km or elongated aspect ratios covering up to 400 km². These out-of-the-box options are balanced to ensure high simulation stability and smooth performance throughout multi-era playthroughs.

### Can mods unlock an experimental transport fever 3 map size on PC?
Yes. On PC, players can modify internal configuration files or download modded scripts to unlock experimental territory dimensions. This allows enthusiasts with high-end rigs to generate Megalomaniac and custom node-based maps reaching 32 km × 32 km (over 1,000 km²), though this requires top-tier CPUs and ample system RAM to prevent simulation slowdowns.

### How does the new node-based terrain system affect map generation?
Rather than applying a single uniform climate across an entire generated area, the node-based system dynamically blends multiple biomes—such as desert valleys, temperate plains, and snowy peaks—seamlessly into one another on the same map. This creates organic natural obstacles and distinct geographic regions that feel unique every time you start a new campaign or sandbox game.

### Will console versions support the same map sizes as the PC version?
PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X will support the standard range of vanilla sizes, including large multi-biome environments. However, console platforms will not support experimental file edits to unlock uncapped map scales, and Xbox Series S may utilize adjusted density settings to maintain steady performance.
