## Getting Started: Campaign vs. Free Play

Before jumping straight into an enormous custom sandbox, newcomers should understand the tools at their disposal. The campaign serves as an interactive, story-focused tutorial that introduces basic mouse controls, route planning, and cargo demands under guided conditions. Early missions present humorous scenarios—ranging from clearing stray wildlife off construction plots to provisioning retro music festivals—that gently teach you how logistics lines interact.

When launching your first map, using this Transport Fever 3 beginners guide will keep you from squandering starting capital on unnecessary infrastructure. Early campaign missions restrict available vehicle types so you can master core concepts like line creation, depot management, and dispatch intervals without financial pressure.

## Mastering Early-Game Transit: The 1900 Era

Your business begins in the year 1900, a period dominated by simple dirt roads and horse-drawn carriages. In this era, private vehicle traffic is non-existent, meaning your delivery lines will not face gridlock from civilian commuters. However, low vehicle speeds and minimal carrying capacities mean profit margins per trip are slim.

A foundational tip in any Transport Fever 3 beginners guide is to respect early timeline mechanics: speed and road condition dictate survival. In early previews, such as [CGMagazine's hands-on look at Transport Fever 3](https://www.cgmagonline.com/articles/previews/transport-fever-3/), players noted that unpaved, poorly maintained routes cause severe vehicle wear. If your carriages or early trucks run over rugged terrain, maintenance costs rise dramatically and vehicles can break down mid-transit.

| Early Infrastructure Type | Initial Build Cost | Maintenance Cost | Speed Limit | Vehicle Breakdown Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dirt Trail / Unpaved Road** | Very Low | Low | 15–20 mph | High (Frequent wear) |
| **Basic Cobblestone / Macadam** | Moderate | Moderate | 25–35 mph | Moderate |
| **Paved Two-Lane Road** | High | Moderate-High | 45–60 mph | Low |
| **Reinforced Industrial Route** | Very High | High | 60+ mph | Negligible |

Upgrading critical routes between production hubs and drop-off stations costs money upfront, but it prevents costly breakdowns and accelerates turnaround times. When laying roads, avoid sharp elevation changes. Steep inclines drastically slow down early low-horsepower vehicles, turning a seemingly short route into a slow, unprofitable trek.

## Establishing Profitable Cargo Supply Chains

Cargo logistics forms the financial backbone of your empire. Raw material extractors (such as logging camps, farms, and oil wells) do not generate goods automatically unless an active, valid delivery line connects them to a processing facility or consumer market with genuine demand.

As highlighted in this Transport Fever 3 beginners guide, mismatched cargo capacity leads to severe cash flow bottlenecks. Always inspect the exact cargo type a vehicle can carry before assigning it to a line. A flatbed wagon carrying construction timber cannot deliver fresh produce or perishable food supplies to a growing town.

```
[Resource Site: Farm / Forest]
               │ (Raw Freight)
               ▼
[Processing Plant: Mill / Food Factory]
               │ (Processed Freight)
               ▼
[Urban Commercial / Industrial Drop-off Zone]
```

| Supply Chain Component | Primary Function | Ideal Vehicle Assignment | Common Beginner Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Extraction Node** | Produces base raw materials (Logs, Grain) | High-capacity bulk haulers | Running empty returns over vast distances |
| **Processing Plant** | Refines raw inputs into finished goods | Specialized cargo carriers | Over-supplying raw input beyond plant capacity |
| **Urban Delivery Hub** | Distributes end products to city retail | Nimble, medium-speed trucks | Placing depots outside commercial catchment zones |
| **Intermodal Station** | Transfers cargo between road, rail, and water | Switchers and automated freight terminals | Inadequate platform length causing line backups |

To maximize route margins, aim for two-way utilization whenever possible. If your trucks deliver raw crops to a processing plant near a town, check if that same plant outputs consumer goods that can be returned to your starting hub. Hauling cargo in both directions effectively cuts your fuel and running expenses in half.

## Designing High-Capacity Passenger Networks

While freight generates steady early capital, passenger transport scales exponentially as urban centers expand. Transport Fever 3 categorizes urban zones into residential, commercial, and industrial districts. Citizens live in residential districts, commute to industrial zones for work, and visit commercial zones for leisure and shopping.

City integration is another critical topic explored in this Transport Fever 3 beginners guide. Placing heavy rail terminals directly in downtown centers often triggers severe demolition expenses and spikes local noise pollution. Instead, place major passenger rail stations on the periphery of the town and shuttle commuters into city centers using localized tram and bus loops.

Unlike earlier titles where subway or sunken stations required third-party modifications, Transport Fever 3 incorporates native underground tram stations. This allows you to construct multi-level transfer stations in dense urban corridors without leveling existing skyscrapers.

| Transit Tier | Best Operational Role | Optimal Stop Spacing | Ideal Infrastructure Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local Feeder** | Neighborhood collection to transit hubs | 200–400 meters | Surface electric trams or local buses |
| **Urban Arterial** | High-density crosstown connections | 500–800 meters | Underground trams or dedicated busways |
| **Regional Commuter** | Connecting neighboring towns | 1,500–3,000 meters | Double-track passenger diesel/electric trains |
| **High-Speed Express**| Long-distance cross-map travel | Map-spanning | Dedicated high-speed rail or regional airliners |

Ensure your local feeder lines run with high frequency. Passengers in Transport Fever 3 evaluate route travel times; if a tram takes too long to arrive, travelers will choose to walk or use personal motor vehicles, depriving your network of fare revenue.

## Financial Management and Fleet Replacement

Running out of liquidity is the fastest route to game over. Every vehicle in your fleet carries ongoing running costs, regardless of whether it is fully loaded, half-full, or sitting motionless at an obstructed terminal. Maintaining healthy financial discipline is the core pillar of this Transport Fever 3 beginners guide.

Keep an eye on vehicle condition. As vehicles age, their maintenance costs steadily creep upward until operating them costs more than purchasing a modern replacement. Set automatic vehicle renewal thresholds in your fleet manager to swap out aging carriages and locomotives once they hit their standard operational lifespan.

| Financial Indicator | Ideal Target Benchmark | Warning Sign | Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operating Margin** | > 35% profit per line | Falling below 10% | Consolidate fleet size; adjust wait intervals |
| **Vehicle Condition** | "Good" to "Very Good" | "Poor" or "Decaying" | Trigger automatic replacement in fleet tab |
| **Station Utilization** | 70–85% cargo capacity | Overflowing / Goods decaying | Add additional transport vehicles or lengthen stops |
| **Loan Interest** | < 5% of monthly revenue | Consistently over 15% | Pay down starting debt before major expansions |

Avoid repaying loans aggressively in the opening five years. Retaining liquid cash gives you the flexibility to terraform efficient rail beds, upgrade bottlenecked roads, and weather sudden resource production pauses without taking emergency loans at punitive rates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are my trucks breaking down so frequently on early routes?
In Transport Fever 3, road surface quality directly influences vehicle wear. Operating heavily loaded cargo wagons over unpaved dirt trails causes rapid wear and tear. Upgrading your transit corridors with paved surfaces and replacing aging vehicles dramatically lowers breakdown rates and reduces ongoing maintenance fees.

### How does this Transport Fever 3 beginners guide help manage line frequency?
Line frequency reflects the average wait time between vehicle arrivals at a given station. By monitoring line frequency in your route overview tab, you can add or remove vehicles until passenger wait times drop, preventing cargo stations from overflowing and keeping commuter satisfaction high.

### Can I share maps with other players in sandbox mode?
Yes. Transport Fever 3 features world generation Seed IDs in Free Play mode. By sharing your specific alphanumeric seed string, other players can generate identical maps with the same terrain contours, city placements, and industrial nodes to compare transit designs.

### Should I complete the campaign before starting Free Play?
While not strictly required, playing through the initial campaign acts as a hands-on tutorial. It introduces core mechanics—such as vehicle purchases, catchment zones, multi-stop routing, and terraforming—in a structured environment with low financial risk, making the leap into large custom maps much smoother.
