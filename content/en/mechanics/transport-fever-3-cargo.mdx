## Understanding Transport Fever 3 Cargo Mechanics and City Demand

Understanding how **transport fever 3 cargo** mechanics function under the hood is critical for maintaining profitable freight networks. Cities in the game demand specific commercial and industrial finished goods—such as tools, food, fuel, machines, construction materials, and goods—to expand their footprint. When you deliver these supplies, the city registers your deliveries and calculates a fulfillment percentage that directly influences population growth.

However, towns do not hold inventory indefinitely. Instead, cities consume delivered freight based on an internal decay timer. If your trains or trucks arrive in massive, infrequent batches, the local supply rating spikes to 100% and then collapses before the next delivery arrives. Maintaining high satisfaction requires a continuous stream of arrivals rather than sporadic mega-convoys.

| City Tier | Typical Population | Required Cargo Types | Target Delivery Cadence | Recommended Primary Vehicle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Small Town** | 100 – 400 | 1 – 2 Goods | Every 90–120 seconds | Medium Delivery Trucks |
| **Growing City** | 500 – 1,200 | 2 – 3 Goods | Every 60–90 seconds | Short Freight Trains / Heavy Trucks |
| **Regional Hub** | 1,300 – 2,000 | 3 – 4 Goods | Every 45–60 seconds | Dedicated Block Trains & Local Distribution |
| **Metropolis** | 2,000+ | All Demanded Goods | Every 30–45 seconds | Double-Tracked Freight Mainlines + Multi-Truck Hubs |

To supply these cities efficiently, production facilities dynamically scale their output based on connected destination capacity. A factory will only ship units if there is valid demand at the final receiver and sufficient transit capacity along the entire logistics chain.

## Designing Scalable Cargo Corridors and Distribution Hubs

When designing high-capacity **transport fever 3 cargo** hubs, separating long-distance freight trains from city-center delivery vehicles is the gold standard. Attempting to bring large freight trains directly into downtown commercial centers creates catastrophic road bottlenecks, destroys valuable commercial real estate, and spikes acoustic pollution.

A hub-and-spoke model resolves these problems cleanly. Bulk trains haul finished goods from distant manufacturing complexes into an off-city intermodal terminal. From there, smaller, clean delivery trucks disperse the freight across designated industrial and commercial loading zones.

```
[Raw Resource Extraction] ---> [Processing Plant] ---> [Manufacturing Factory]
                                                                |
                                                      (Heavy Rail Corridor)
                                                                v
[City Center Stores] <--- (Clean Electric Trucks) <--- [Out-of-Town Cargo Hub]
```

Choosing the correct mode of transport for each segment of your logistics web prevents traffic gridlock and maximizes company profits.

| Transport Mode | Optimal Distance | Best Used For | Key Advantage | Main Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cargo Rail** | Medium to Very Long | Bulk resources, finished goods | Enormous throughput per line | High initial infrastructure capital |
| **Road Haulage** | Short to Medium | Final-mile city drops, early game | Unmatched routing flexibility | Causes urban gridlock at high volume |
| **Inland Waterways**| Long (flat terrain) | Raw minerals, oil, mass steel | Lowest running cost per ton | Rigid pathing limited by navigable rivers |
| **Air Cargo** | Extreme Long / Islands| High-value low-weight finished goods | Rapid point-to-point transit | Minimal capacity and extreme running costs |

For multi-platform rail hubs, configure platform assignments manually. Assigning dedicated platforms to specific lines avoids signal standoffs where two trains block an entire terminal neck while waiting for an occupied berth.

## Optimizing Delivery Timers and Preventing Supply Line Starvation

Maintaining consistent delivery intervals is vital for **transport fever 3 cargo** flow. The game's economic simulation calculates fulfillment based on recent receipts rather than annual aggregates. If your factory produces 1,200 units of food annually, but you haul it using a single massive train that visits once every four months, the city will experience alternating periods of oversupply and complete starvation.

To keep ratings stable, configure line capacity to match factory output while minimizing headways. Running three 6-car trains spaced evenly along a line delivers a steady flow of goods, maintaining city satisfaction far more effectively than one 18-car behemoth.

| Issue Diagnosed | Visible In-Game Symptom | Underlying Cause | Corrective Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cargo Decay** | City demand drops to 0% between deliveries | Headway between train arrivals is too long | Replace mega-trains with smaller, frequent services |
| **Station Overcrowding** | Cargo evaporates from platform with warning icon | Station storage buffer exceeded | Expand platform length or add cargo waiting sheds |
| **Factory Bottleneck** | Primary industry halts production unexpectedly | Downstream receiving terminal has backed up | Clear transit blockages or add intermediate storage |
| **Truck Congestion** | Final-mile delivery trucks stopped in urban traffic | Delivery route crosses busy commuter corridors | Build dedicated cargo bypasses or ring roads |

Monitor your line statistics window regularly. Pay close attention to the **Frequency** and **Rate** metrics. Your line rate should slightly exceed the receiving city's consumption rate, while frequency should stay under 90 seconds for maximum supply score stability.

## Managing Late-Game Metropolises Without Track Congestion

In sprawling late-game saves, **transport fever 3 cargo** demand can easily overwhelm regional rail networks. When three or four major cities cluster together, running independent freight links to every single industry clogs junctions and brings passenger express networks to an absolute standstill.

To prevent total network paralysis, construct dedicated grade-separated freight bypasses around major passenger terminals. Flyovers and dive-unders eliminate conflicting diamond crossings, allowing high-speed passenger expresses to cross busy freight corridors without pausing.

| Network Bottleneck | Impact on Freight Flow | Engineering Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Flat Junction Conflicts** | Trains wait at signals, delaying deliveries | Construct flying junctions (bridges/tunnels for left turns) |
| **Platform Throat Contention** | Incoming trains queue on mainlines outside hubs | Add arrival run-around loops and dual-track yard throats |
| **Urban Delivery Gridlock** | City trucks trapped behind passenger bus lines | Implement one-way cargo alleys and underground loading bays |
| **Mixed Speed Conflicts** | 60 mph freight trains delay 125 mph passenger trains | Lay quad-track mainlines (dedicated inner express, outer freight) |
| **Terminal Platform Starvation** | Empty trains block bays waiting for slow cargo loads | Set loading orders to "Load If Available" instead of "Wait Full" |

By decoupling your freight corridors from local passenger traffic, you ensure that even when a metropolis demands thousands of units of commercial goods, your regional transportation empire remains punctual, profitable, and smooth.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Why does my Transport Fever 3 cargo rating drop even though factories are producing goods?
City satisfaction in the game is tied directly to delivery frequency and consumption timers. If your delivery intervals are too long, the city consumes all delivered stock before the next shipment arrives, causing the satisfaction rating to plummet. To resolve this, run smaller vehicles with shorter headways rather than one oversized train.

### How do I stop cargo from vanishing off station platforms?
Cargo items disappear when a platform's physical storage limit is exceeded for an extended duration. To fix station overcrowding, lengthen your cargo platforms, place modular cargo storage buildings on the station footprint, or add more transport capacity to clear goods faster.

### Can you build functional shunting and classification yards in Transport Fever 3?
While players can construct visual rail yards using sidings and waypoint routing, the base game's automated dispatching handles cargo distribution automatically on designated lines. Attempting complex yard switching can introduce substantial delays that harm strict delivery timers, though dedicated logistics mods and careful waypoint management allow for prototypical operations.

### What is the most profitable cargo chain to set up early in a campaign?
Simple, high-volume two-step processing chains—such as Grain/Livestock to Food, or Crude Oil to Fuel—are the most profitable starting ventures. They require minimal intermediate infrastructure, utilize specialized but affordable rolling stock, and allow you to quickly generate the capital needed to construct complex four-stage machinery and tool networks.
